Graphs on the dashboard, and what they are allowed to claim

Sales, gross profit, bookings, enquiries and payment mix, over a window the
user picks. Inline SVG, not a charting library: the library needed here is a
few hundred kilobytes to draw two lines and some bars, and it brings opinions
about scaling that would then have to be fought. The scale is the part that
matters, because a chart that does not start at zero misleads and a library
makes that easy to do by accident.

Three things the numbers say out loud rather than letting a reader assume.

Revenue is net of refunds. Grossing it up would mean the graph disagreed with
the till, which is how people stop believing a graph.

"Profit" is labelled gross profit, and the panel says what it excludes:
wages, rent, running costs. It is not what the clinic keeps. Treatments carry
no cost of goods here, so the margin is flattered -- stated next to the
number rather than left for someone to work out. An owner seeing 90% and
concluding they had a good month would not be wrong about the arithmetic and
very wrong about the business.

Conversion is null, not zero, when nothing came in. Zero percent is a claim
about performance; "nobody asked" is a claim about volume.

Days with no activity come back as zeros, not gaps. A SQL DATE() group over a
window with no sales returns nothing at all, and a chart that silently starts
a fortnight late is worse than no chart.

Two bugs the tests caught, both worth having found:

A refund given on a day with no sales vanished from the daily series, because
the subtraction was folded into the loop over sales. The headline total was
right and the bars did not add up to it. Refunds are now applied on their own
pass.

A CarbonImmutable::addDay() returns a new instance, so a for-loop that
advanced it in place never terminated. It locked the test database and left
eight orphaned PHP processes holding it. The reassignment is commented so the
next person does not tidy it back into a hang.

Each part of the block is gated on its own permission: money on accounting,
takings on pos, the rest on appointments or leads. A receptionist gets
bookings and enquiries without the owner's books.
