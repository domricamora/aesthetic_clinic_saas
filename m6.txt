Faces and emoji in the chat

A long thread was two columns of text with nothing to say who said what, which
matters most exactly when it is hard to follow: a visitor and two staff all in
one conversation. Each message now carries an avatar, tinted by side, and the
bubble has one rounded corner pulled in toward the speaker so the two read as
one shape rather than two stacked bars.

Avatars fall back in three steps -- photo, then initials from the name, then
a generic person. The third is the common case for the first few messages,
since nobody has introduced themselves yet.

Emoji are a fixed set of 64 rather than a picker library. Most of those are
several hundred kilobytes for a grid of symbols a front desk never reaches
for. These insert at the caret rather than the end of the box, so one lands
mid-sentence where you expected it. Same set on both the visitor widget and
the desk reply box.

Worth noting: the 2000 limit counts characters and the column is utf8mb4, so
an emoji costs one of the budget and not four bytes. Both facts are now
pinned by a test -- the round trip reads back off the database rather than
trusting the response, so a mangled byte would fail there instead of in a
browser.
